# Dramatic craft: direct the audience's experience

Read for narrative shorts and drama before Treatment, casting, scene rehearsal and coverage; return
when an assembled scene is coherent but feels flat. Use Brief, Treatment, source Analysis/Timeline,
shot rows and adoption records as owners. Do not create another production database or require a
particular genre, cast size, dialogue density, shot count, palette, score or duration.

For source-led work, [source deconstruction](source-deconstruction.md) owns the whole-work
structure and evidence-based improvement diagnosis; relate local craft to cumulative choices,
costs, patterns and payoffs. Use its repetition/over-explanation diagnosis when scenes are
individually valid but the whole commissioned work feels uniform. Do not add a separate review.

When dialogue, action or exposition lacks a viable performed form, compare the relevant
[scene-expression alternatives](storyboard-craft.md#场次表现备选) against the actual source/target
obligations. New tactics/coverage go to the designated writer; do not fix flatness by deleting
dialogue, mechanically adding walks/cutaways or accelerating every beat.

## Establish the attraction before translating the plot

State the intended viewing experience in concrete terms: whose predicament draws attention, what
the viewer anticipates, which relationship supplies pleasure or discomfort, and what changes at
the payoff. A theme or synopsis does not answer those questions. For original work derive them
from the brief; for adaptation establish them from the actual source before target drafting.

Audit the source detail catalogue against the whole scoped passage and its relevant neighbors,
not only against entries already selected for transfer. Check for omitted attractions: a short
exchange that defines a partnership, an apparently redundant question that earns hope, an active
listener, a striking silhouette, a contrast between extraordinary appearance and ordinary conduct,
a title passage, a sound motif or a designed pause. These examples are possibilities, not quotas.
Look specifically between catalogue intervals as well as inside them. Document meaningful
omissions, unknowns and scoped changes before freezing the catalogue.

Judge removal of a line by what it removes from the scene: information, tactic, permission,
relationship, suspense, comedy, emotional reversal or atmosphere. Shorter wording can be better,
but fewer lines do not establish efficiency. If an adaptation removes a source's distinctive form,
choose and verify an equivalent attraction or declare an authorized redesign; do not assume a
new costume or setting supplies the lost effect.

For the visual/directing choice itself, use [aesthetic direction](aesthetic-direction.md):
accessible examples, viewer position/subtext, an organizing image/sound relationship and
actual comparison. Do not let a checklist or aesthetic label write the scene.

## Cast characters and relationships, not job labels

For consequential characters, choose the few features that make them recognizable in this work:

| Decision | Make it observable |
| --- | --- |
| Desire and pressure | What they want from this encounter; what prevents the easy answer |
| Public tactic and private attitude | How they try to get it, and what their conduct leaks or conceals |
| Physical signature | Silhouette, posture, material, prop use or a characteristic response with a story reason |
| Vocal signature, when speaking | Audible texture, articulation, rhythm and a way of addressing this other person |
| Relationship | Who initiates, completes, obstructs, corrects or changes the other's behavior |
| Change | Which stimulus makes the familiar behavior stop, reverse or acquire another meaning |

A supporting character can be memorable through an economical action or contrast, without a
backstory speech or added subplot. Functional division alone does not establish a partnership.
Choose a behavior that affects the exchange and can later change; avoid an arbitrary tic attached
to every line. Distinct voices need not mean caricatured accents, extreme pitches or effects.
Do not impose uniqueness on anonymous extras whose function is collective presence.

For a cast/location transformation, ask what previously made the people and world special and
what now carries that appeal. Believability, restraint, beauty and naturalism are separate choices;
none is synonymous with professionalism. Stylized, broad, intimate, comic and expressionistic work
can each meet the brief. Do not turn fear of generation defects into a neutral aesthetic.

Test key casting in an actual exchange with a listener and relevant action when a new identity,
voice or performance direction is unsettled. A portrait accepts appearance; a successful isolated
line does not accept the partnership, voice across blocks or scene. Reuse valid trials within the
authorized budget rather than creating an additional audition ritual for every settled character.
Read [voice direction](../playbooks/craft/voice-direction.md) for speech casting and supported samples.

## Write beats as changes, then rehearse their time

Use [adaptation and dialogue](adaptation-and-dialogue.md) for the ordinary-speech/comprehension pass:
characters respond to what they actually heard; distinct habits and tactics survive translation.
A concise thematic maxim or complete rule explanation may be clear to its author yet unnatural or
opaque to its listener. Test without the world bible or director's rationale.

In existing scene/shot rows, connect entry state, stimulus, objective/tactic, observable response
and exit state. State what changes for the character and viewer, not just what sentence is spoken.
Suspense may change through withheld information; comedy through expectation and response; a quiet
scene through attention or relationship. Every second need not deliver a new fact or a reversal.

Translate private feelings into performable behavior at the intended shot size. Give actors
actions toward another person: probe, dismiss, conceal, reassure, test or withhold, as applicable.
An adjective such as tired, calm or angry does not define the action. Direct listeners too. Keep
meaningful onset, peak and release, without forcing a facial sequence or a fixed reaction hold.
Check negative instructions against positive obligations: no exaggerated acting, no smile, little
movement or no music must not erase a required expressive register.

Only when timing/readability remains uncertain, use [scene rehearsal](scene-design.md#rehearse-only-unresolved-timing-and-readability)
for the affected complete sequence with scratch dialogue, action/reaction space and necessary cues.
Reuse the source timing and accepted trials elsewhere; do not require two passes or a full-film animatic. Silent or wordless work uses its intended action/sound clock. Incorporate title
passages when they structure the scene. A collection of accepted stills or a duration spreadsheet
is not a scene rehearsal. Scratch timing guides generation; actual accepted performance establishes
the adopted clock unless the verified route uses authoritative exact audio.

When comparing rhythm, align meaningful beats rather than equal timestamps. Distinguish:

- **Plot progression:** how soon a fact, decision or consequence arrives.
- **Dramatic density:** changes of tactic, relationship, expectation, feeling or attention.
- **Performed rhythm:** speech, interruption, listening, action, settling and expressive silence.
- **Editorial rhythm:** shot relationships, focus changes, sound bridges, repetition and release.

Word counts, speech gaps and shot lengths locate questions; they do not award quality. An earlier
payoff can still feel slow if the route to it has lost meaningful exchanges. A long hold can be
compelling if the viewer's expectation continues to develop. Do not solve flatness automatically
with speed-up, more cuts, camera motion, music or louder reactions.

[Attraction and rhythm](attention-and-rhythm.md) owns active quiet, varied narrative energy, complete
AV rehearsal and source-beat comparison. Preserve concrete humor/curiosity/contrast where applicable;
do not replace them all with low-energy dialogue and interchangeable holds. Different genres need
different pleasures, not a compulsory joke or fixed modern cutting rate.

For an exact duration, make time fit the intended experience during rehearsal. Separate authored,
requested and adopted seconds, including title passages and the reaction after the final line.
Do not reserve only enough time to finish the last syllable. A hard duration is a delivery
constraint, not a reason to spread thin dialogue over empty holds or cut the emotional consequence.

## Design picture, sound and writing as one expression

Use [cinematic look](cinematic-look.md) and [style lock](style-lock.md) to choose what this scene
needs from space, light, perspective, focus and materials. A generic cinematic label, shallow focus,
low saturation, grain or delivery resolution does not supply a photographic argument. Art and lens
choices should make the attention, relationship and world legible; keep motivated variation.

Plan coverage for the viewer's discovery and the person affected by it. A prop insert can establish
evidence, but cannot routinely replace the reaction that gives that evidence meaning. Choose a
cut by what attention changes across it, not merely by speaker change or a generation boundary.
J/L cuts, a held two-shot, parallel action and reaction coverage are available choices, not rules.

Sound has dramatic jobs as well as levels. Use the existing cue plan to choose vocal relationships,
room perspective, physical contact, off-screen presence, recurring motifs, meaningful absence and
entry/change/withdrawal of score when applicable. Not every work needs music or additional stems.
Repeated character IDs or textual voice adjectives do not lock vocal identity; verify actual input
capabilities and accepted outputs. Do not claim an unbound audio reference or import an unsupported
generative dubbing/lip-sync route. See [sound and mix](../playbooks/craft/sound-mix.md).

Treat a source-led or deliberately designed title as a passage when it organizes the work:
dramatic trigger → image/negative space → typography and reading hierarchy → appearance/hold/exit
→ sound relationship → next-shot handoff. A corner label, subtitle, full-frame title or omission can
each be right for a brief; do not downgrade an important source title by default to avoid covering
faces. Design a suitable image and type together. Typography needs actual glyph, material, scale,
contrast and animation choices, not just a large font. Captions, diegetic writing and titles have
different jobs. Test proxy titles in rehearsal and the actual encoded passage at delivery size.

## Finish defects without spending the scene's meaning

For a cutaway, static insert, crop or replacement, name both its repair job and its dramatic cost:
which action, voice, expression or discovery it covers; what remains visible; when attention returns;
and whether the needed setup/payoff/reaction still survives. Repeated stills are not automatically
bad, but must earn their reading time and changing meaning. Correct lettering does not itself
establish a persuasive prop, photographic integration or a successful scene.

If removing an unusable smile, pose or speech fragment also removes a critical beat, obtain usable
coverage through the permitted route and authorized budget, redesign within the agreed scope, or
keep the gap explicit. Do not call concealed failure a preserved performance. Review repairs with
neighbors and reopen the affected conditions when actual media changes.

Judge the whole assembled work at intended speed and sound without reading production explanations.
Ask where attention drifts, what makes each consequential character identifiable, whether the
expected emotion actually develops, and whether the payoff and ending have time to register.
Actual user/target-viewer feedback is evidence when available; predicted reactions remain predictions.
Follow [artistic review](../production/short-drama-production.md) and [scene acceptance](../production/scene-acceptance.md).
Unavailable motion/audio inspection leaves that modality unverified; provide a provisional preview
with exact remaining gates, not a professional audiovisual acceptance inferred from grids or ASR.

## Method basis and limits

The [Yale Film Analysis editing guide](https://filmanalysis.yale.edu/editing/) explains editing as
relationships directing attention and shaping experienced time, including expressive holds and
matches. Its [sound guide](https://filmanalysis.yale.edu/sound/) distinguishes source, perspective
and sound bridges. Pages were read on 2026-10-10. The decisions above adapt those analytical
principles to Hypit's existing production owners; they are not copied film examples, model-specific
performance claims or evidence that this workflow has passed an end-to-end generation benchmark.
