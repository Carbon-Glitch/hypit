# Scene design: make the scene readable before committing to video

For short drama, use this after Brief and Treatment to design space, scale, performance and state.
The scene is the unit of dramatic judgment; generation blocks supply its material. Use the existing
Treatment, asset ledger and shot table as owners. The row shapes below are examples to adapt, not
another mandatory set of documents.

For recreation, derive scene direction from the [fidelity contract](fidelity-contract.md) and source
detail evidence. The performance chain must retain distinctive gesture/gait, intensity, listening
responses and atmosphere, not merely the sequence of plot turns. Preserve a source close-up or find
equally readable coverage for its specific reaction; a wide dialogue shot is not automatically enough.

## Establish space and scale

For requested cinematic quality, assign art and photographic decisions using
[cinematic look](cinematic-look.md): light motivation and world-space source, exposure/attention
hierarchy, focus plane, material response and allowed scene variation. Moving actors must inhabit
that lighting; reverse shots project the same physical sources into their own views. Settle actual
scene frames as well as a geometry plan before batching video. Bright comedy and broad source
acting need not become dark or restrained to satisfy the photographic target.

Before formal shots for a materially ambiguous high-value scene, use
[director planning](../production/director-planning.md) to compare viable coverage and knowledge
timing within the preservation contract. Ordinary or already settled scenes need no audition.
Record show-now/withhold-now facts, their carriers and release triggers; camera/sound choices must
not prematurely reveal what the source holds or hide evidence the scene needs to deliver.

An empty-location reference does not record who is in the room. Establish a scene overview: doors,
windows, useful furniture, characters, extras or crowd zones, camera positions and the axis of
action. Give recurring camera views IDs so a return shot reuses the same geography. Screen-left is
camera-relative; world positions, eyelines and movement direction must agree across views. Crossing
an axis can be intentional when camera movement or an establishing view makes it legible.

Where relative size matters, use a same-plane standing figure showing builds and relative heights,
then check seated and moving shots against furniture and camera height. Do not measure height drift
by comparing head pixels from different perspectives. A shared frame needs credible proportions,
not a centimeter number pasted into every prompt.

For difficult geometry, use a rough composite or simple 3D layout to design accepted shot
compositions. An image sent as a generic reference still only guides generation.
[Request controls](../production/request-controls.md) distinguishes that from an actual first-frame
input. Select references for the current camera and state; do not send every dossier view or a
conflicting outfit just because slots remain available.

Track crowds at the level the story exposes: scene-wide presence and occupied zones, plus recognizable
background people when repeated views make them noticeable. Off-screen does not mean absent. A room
becoming empty needs a motivated exit or a legible time jump.

## Direct a causal performance

Use [dramatic craft](dramatic-craft.md) to establish consequential characters' behavior, vocal
signature when applicable and relationships beyond task division. Choose the scene's attraction
and tactics before requesting neutral coverage. Restraint is a register, not the removal of
response; broad/stylized source performance remains eligible. Character casting and an actual
exchange with listening/action are different judgments from accepting portrait identity.

Write the chain for the scene and its important turns:

> entry state → stimulus → what the character stops or notices → objective and tactic → visible
> or audible response → exit state and what remains unresolved.

Choose behavior readable at the shot size. A reaction may be a stopped hand, delayed answer or
withheld glance; it need not be a smile, grimace or multiple facial cues. Timing follows the performed
language and beat, not a fixed reaction duration. Separate private emotion from public behavior.
A polite smile hiding refusal needs a readable cause and leak; review cannot invent that motivation
after generation.

In a continuous exchange, inherit the previous accepted emotional and physical state. Across a time
jump, flashback, montage or new scene, retain persistent facts and establish what changed. Do not
require every scene to start at the previous scene's emotional intensity.

Use recorded rehearsal or a performance reference when timing or restraint is hard to direct in
words. Verify whether the route transfers face, hands, body or speech: support for one does not
establish all. Inspect for source-actor or source-scene leakage before scaling up.

## Keep meaningful state transitions

Use the compact @continuity marker in the existing 26-column risk cell for consequential space/hand/prop dependencies; see [runtime state handoff](../production/spatial-continuity.md). Static geography comes from the accepted 3/4 overview; dynamic facts come from actual adopted observations. Preserve offscreen facts and derive screen placement from the current camera. A changed location/state image must not be contradicted by an old upright/unbroken/closed reference. No extra continuity screenplay is created.

Relevant shots record presence, positions and eyelines, outfit version, prop holder and hand, prop
condition, action phase and emotional tactic at entry and exit. Include off-screen facts that will
become visible again; avoid fields with no consequence for this scene.

| Shot / camera | Entry facts | Stimulus and action | Exit facts | Transition / evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SH12 / CAM-B | Teacher seated left; lead holds folder right-handed; class present | Name called; lead stops closing folder and looks up | Folder open; lead guarded | Continuous to SH13; adopted clip and selected out time |
| SH13 / CAM-A | Same people and folder state, reverse view | Task announced; lead starts to refuse | Teacher interrupts the unfinished reply | Cut on interruption; no added smile |
| SH14 / CAM-C | Later, in costume; same identity | First look in mirror | Attempts to compose herself | Ellipsis established by location and outfit reveal |

There is no universal one-step limit. Check whether the change is motivated and visible or reasonably
implied. An insert can cover a hand movement; it cannot silently reverse a crucial prop transfer or
conceal the cause of the story's reversal. Record deliberate omissions so review neither misclassifies
them as errors nor excuses unplanned jumps.

## Rehearse only unresolved timing and readability

Reuse the analyzed source timing
and accepted project evidence. Rehearse only a changed-language, contact, spatial or reveal sequence
whose timing/readability is uncertain; skip a separate animatic for settled passages. The first actual
usable H3 result can test the unknown and enter the cut, rather than producing a disposable pilot.

When rehearsal is needed, assemble the smallest complete setup→action→payoff→reaction with existing
images/proxies, scratch dialogue and necessary sound/music/title cues. It must be playable; stills or
a duration table alone do not demonstrate rhythm. A full-film pre-score is optional for a genuinely
uncertain cumulative arc, not a mandatory artifact. Keep its observations in the existing scene rows.

Separate authored edit duration, requested generation duration and actual adopted interval. Complete
speech, non-overlapping actions and readable reactions must fit; request limits do not set final shot
length. Static rehearsal cannot prove performance, motion, contact or lip sync. Verify those on actual
H3 footage under the existing budget, then continue from usable results.

Update owning rows when adopted footage differs from the plan. Repair unintended contradictions
before dependent work uses them.

Use [spatial continuity](../production/spatial-continuity.md) to cross-check actual master panels,
recurring camera views, conflicting positive inputs and dependency-aware scheduling. A declared
axis is not enough when both reverse views regenerate the same impossible background. Export the
current selected entry facts and actual predecessor state into the
[current shot/queue records](../production/short-drama-production.md); dynamically dependent shots do not batch
from an unchanged generic hero frame. [Attraction and rhythm](attention-and-rhythm.md) governs the
complete rehearsal and editorial clock, so geography control does not flatten the whole scene.
