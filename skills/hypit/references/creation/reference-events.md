# Event-level reference reading

Use for short-drama reversal. Put shared/E/EX records in SOURCE_CONTEXT.json and link them from the canonical table;
reuse existing useful records without rewriting them. The [25-column source-shot table](source-shot-analysis.md) records every shot across the full
observation dimensions; events expand important phases inside those rows and across cuts. Record observations before interpreting or redesigning them.

Apply the [fidelity contract](fidelity-contract.md). A plot event groups multiple detail observations;
do not collapse them into a single row if they differ in action, reaction, coverage or sound. Record
mouth/eye/brow behavior, posture and gait, physical contact, bystander response and stylized devices
where they carry the source's attraction. Ordinary and exaggerated registers are equally valid.

## Four reading scales

1. Inspect the entire episode's picture and the text/audio evidence selected by
   [source audio mode](../production/source-audio-policy.md) for premise, attraction, information
   and power changes, escalation, payoff and unanswered questions. Analysis distinguishes
   interpretations and uninspected sound; later target sound never becomes an observed source fact.
2. Read each scene for geography, camera sides, presence, objectives, tactics and emotional causality.
3. Verify every shot boundary and read its internal development, not only its start/end pose:
   attention, speech/action/listening phases, changing knowledge/relationship and its neighbors.
   A generation block is not a source shot or a required target edit length.
4. Inspect critical events densely: stimulus, reaction onset, preparation, contact, release, reveal,
   settling and the actual cut. Use short normal-speed playback, larger frames and every-frame
   inspection where ambiguity matters. A fixed two-second grid cannot establish those events.

Do not inspect every frame of every uneventful interval mechanically. Do account for the whole source
through playback and record where dense checks were necessary. Samples cannot prove their gaps.

The [source audio mode](../production/source-audio-policy.md) can limit current reading to full
picture and subtitle/transcript evidence. Keep original sound unknown where uninspected; the
sound portion of this method then describes later work, not a current inspection claim.
Visual internal phases and known experience obligations still need evidence. New target sound
is an authored choice; do not backfill it into original event rows.

## Coverage and uncertainty

| Source interval | Watched/listened evidence | Dense inspection and reason | Unresolved fact / next evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| start–end | Clip/file and source time; video/audio separately | Cut, handoff, expression or reveal range | State unknown; do not guess |

If sound cannot be heard or motion cannot be played, mark that dimension unverified. OCR is not spoken
dialogue; ASR is not speaker attribution. Do not invent missing frames, exact lens millimeters, light
measurements or camera coordinates from a compressed image. Separate visible geometry/focus effects
from inferred technique and from parameters later chosen for the target.

## Read the experience unfolding inside each shot

Every source shot gets a full [source-shot row](source-shot-analysis.md), with verified time,
all dimensions checked, its dramatic/attention job, incoming/outgoing state and evidence limits.
Expand important internal changes into event records at the evidence-supported precision. A shot
number, synopsis, dialogue list or two endpoint images cannot substitute for the behavior between
them. A genuinely held/static interval can be recorded as such; do not invent movement or require
a fixed number of events or sampled frames. Unknown/inapplicable dimensions remain explicit.

Event records derive from specific SH rows: distinguish stimulus, reaction onset, preparation,
contact/reveal, result and settling where they matter, retaining simultaneous listeners and cues.
Use the chronological view in source-shot-analysis when several changes would otherwise collapse
into one emotion adjective. The SH row links E details rather than copying their entire text.

| Inspect | Recover the relationship, not just a label |
| --- | --- |
| Cut and entry/exit | What is carried into this shot; the visible/audible event on which it cuts; whether a voice, action or expectation continues into its neighbor |
| Performer and listener | Stimulus, response latency, observable onset/peak/release and settling; simultaneous gaze, hand/posture/contact behavior; who initiates, completes, interrupts or withholds |
| Speech and sound | Actual speaking turns and on/off-screen ownership, emphasis, phrasing, hesitation/overlap; breath/contact/ambience/score entries, changes and meaningful absence |
| Picture and art | Where attention begins/moves, subject scale and occlusion, depth/focus/camera behavior, significant silhouette/prop/light/material changes and readable physical consequences |
| Writing and effects | Exact visible content, diegetic versus audience/subjective layer, trigger, appearance/scale/intensity, hold/release and sound relationship; critical phases of titles and designed effects |
| Information and feeling | What character/viewer knows or anticipates before/after; inferred tactic, hope, tension, humor or release tied to the observed form and its timing |

For a consequential exchange or reveal, retain a compact chronology in the existing event rows:

| Source time or word/action trigger | Picture and simultaneous behavior | Speech/sound | Inferred attention/emotion change | Evidence and uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Actual address/trigger | Observed phase, actor/listener/camera/prop | Actually heard cue and its relation to the image | Evidence-supported reading, not a claimed inner state | Clip/frames/audio and what remains unknown |

Check the preceding and succeeding shots at intended speed and sound. A response can start before
the cut, a reply can begin over a listener, or a payoff can depend on a delayed return to a face.
Do not force picture, speaker turn and dramatic beat to share one boundary. Source seconds locate
evidence; target-language performance must re-establish these relationships rather than blindly
copying pause lengths. Densify sampling around ambiguous changes/cuts and inspect at useful size;
normal playback remains necessary for performance and rhythm, and direct listening for sound.

Separate three claims in existing records: **observed form**, **inferred dramatic intention and
invited viewer experience**, and **unknown**. Explain how the framing/action/cut/sound supports an
interpretation; retain plausible alternatives when the evidence is ambiguous. Unless supported
by supplied testimony, do not claim knowledge of the director's private intent. Invited feeling
is not proof that every viewer felt it; actual feedback remains separate evidence. Nonessential
unknown lens/technique facts need not block work, but missing critical action, sound or expressive
evidence cannot be replaced with an invented explanation.

## Scene record

For professional photographic targets, attach reference-backed scene/shot look observations and
quality conditions from [cinematic look](cinematic-look.md): space/focus/light/material and their
changes during movement. Keep source facts separate from new target production requirements; do
not rewrite the source catalogue to claim a newly chosen camera or grade was observed in the source.

For reveals, record each fact separately: character knowledge, audience knowledge, current visual
or audible carrier, what remains withheld and the release trigger. Face identity, spoken relationship
and prop contents may reveal at different times. This feeds the target shot's information permission
in [director planning](../production/director-planning.md).

Record camera/view IDs, world positions and axis, extras' occupied zones, scale relationships,
physical light sources, observed palette/texture, recurring voice qualities and continuing sound
beds. Note which facts persist beyond the scene. An unseen person remains present until evidence
establishes an exit. A crowd needs individually tracked identities only where recognizability matters.

## Event record

Use stable IDs such as SC01.SH03.E02. Split an event when it changes story knowledge, action state,
performance tactic or edit timing. Keep meaningful simultaneous actions together or explicitly link
them. Do not reduce every background blink to a production requirement.

| Field | Required observation when relevant |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | Source interval, clip/frame/word address, verified or uncertain |
| Participants | Actor, recipient, listener and simultaneous background actions |
| Trigger | Exact word, sound, gaze, contact or discovery that initiates the event |
| Action phases | Preparation → contact/action → release/result; hand, holder, quantity and prop state |
| Performance | Objective/tactic as interpretation; observed gaze, posture, face, breath, latency and restraint |
| Picture attention | Framing, subject scale, screen/world position, focus target/change, occlusion, camera path and pace |
| Speech | Verbatim speaker-attributed words; on/off-screen status, stress, pause, interruption and overlap |
| Sound | Timed physical/character sounds and designed accents; continuous ambience and score changes separately |
| Handoff | Incoming state, adopted outgoing state, motion/sound carried across the cut |
| Story function | Who learns what, power shift, setup/payoff or contrast; critical, supporting or incidental |

For each production-relevant detail, also record: observable form (what the body/face/camera/sound
actually does), expressive effect (interpretation tied to the evidence), required visibility or
audibility, and the concrete loss that would weaken it. A long scene-wide row is an index, not a
replacement for these detail records. No fixed count or expression list substitutes for source reading.

For critical events, start/end descriptions alone are insufficient when the middle carries meaning.
A hand moving from empty to holding an envelope must preserve the transfer if that transfer is the
reveal. A listener's reaction must be tied to the stimulus, not just tagged angry or surprised.

A useful hypothetical record is: the lead holds the folder open; on hearing that the costume is
already prepared, her closing hand stops; she begins to object; the teacher interrupts before the
reply finishes; she keeps her guarded gaze. Record such a sequence only if observed. Its timing in
an English adaptation must be re-established from the English performance, not invented from this
example or copied from the original seconds.

## Cross-shot experience units

Relate units to the [whole-work structure](source-deconstruction.md#全片和跨集结构): scene contribution, cumulative choice/cost, serial obligations and audiovisual development. Keep that account once in SOURCE_CONTEXT, linking EX/detail IDs.

A collection of correct detail rows can still lose the experience formed by their order,
withholding, repetition or aftermath. SOURCE_CONTEXT owns the compact experience index; SOURCE_SHOTS owns
its original detail evidence. Use stable IDs such as EX01, linking observed source passages
rather than copying their prose. These are audience/causal units, not source shots or H3 blocks.

| Unit / source span / detail IDs | Entering viewer and character position | Setup → expectation/misreading → change/reveal → reaction → residue | Evidence-supported intention/experience hypothesis | Necessity / dependencies / uncertainty | Target condition and loss signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Record which concrete cue first plants a question or hope, when viewers/characters can know
the answer, whose response registers it, and what carries beyond the last line. Include distant
setups/payoffs, motifs, persistent relationship/knowledge changes and the next unit's prerequisite.
Use only the phases this unit actually has; a held feeling need not invent a reversal, and every
scene need not express all six headline emotions. Inspect meaningful source intervals missing
from every selected detail, not just the gaps inside recorded events.

Test the interpretation against the actual sequence: what would removing/reordering this cue
change in attention, expectation or consequence, and what competing reading fits the evidence?
A counterfactual is a directing hypothesis, not new observed evidence. Private intent requires
supplied testimony; claimed audience response requires actual feedback. Resolve uncertainty
that changes the core obligation before freezing; nonessential production guesses can remain unknown.

Treatment maps each inherited EX ID to its target mechanism and separately testable condition;
SCREENPLAY/STORYBOARD carry the ordered setup/change/response and their dependencies. Several
H3 jobs, pickups or post cues may realize one unit. Allocate coverage across them, including
what must survive the chosen edit; declaring every detail present somewhere is insufficient.
Use EX IDs in existing experience/acceptance conditions and owner links, not new provider fields
or a parallel JSON registry. The v2 transfer `unit` remains its execution-block ID.

Rehearse the complete unit and adjoining state, then inspect its actual selected/exported
sequence. Record target-only clarity and unit completeness as well as aligned source comparison.
Missing preparation, premature disclosure, cut-off listener response or erased residue fails
that essential unit even when every dialogue or identity check passed.

## Hand off facts without losing them

Before new adapted video generation, the current executor checks this reading
against original scoped playback, including relevant neighbors, before judging the target plan.
Check every indexed shot and relevant internal events, catalogue gaps, concurrent listeners,
sound/graphics and the interpretation boundaries above. The creator's complete-looking table is
not proof of complete understanding. See the enforced
[source-reading audit](../production/short-drama-production.md).

During the same source-analysis pass, compare the records with source playback and verify every
shot is indexed, important phases and listener reactions are represented, and sound/style have not
been inferred solely from stills. Mark unknowns. Keep source truth separate from the target plan; never reconstruct it from the adapted manifest.
Corrections reopen only affected target rows, requests and observations. See [field mapping](../production/detail-transfer-format.md).

Mark critical events explicitly and give uncertain critical facts an evidence task before adaptation.
Keep supporting details that make the event readable; label incidental details so they need not all
be repeated in every prompt. Target decisions preserve or replace contributions within the agreed scope; omission needs an
explicitly changed preservation scope, not just a feasibility reason. [Story transfer](../production/story-transfer.md) owns those decisions and their destinations.
