# Style lock: a production-specific visual agreement

Derive the look from the reference and Brief. VISUAL owns the global visual manual and palettes;
ASSETS owns actual files, versions and acceptance, while Treatment links and explains choices.
Style is a reusable decision, not proof that a model will reproduce it exactly.

For professional photographic work, [cinematic look development](cinematic-look.md) supplies the
specific scene brief and static/motion quality gates. Pin accepted light/exposure, perspective/focus,
material and color relationships with actual scene images; do not accept a film label as the brief.
The look target is source/Brief-backed, not the most successful earlier rough cut. Record failure
signals and legitimate scene variations in Treatment; chosen hero frames alone do not prove motion.

For recreation, the [fidelity contract](fidelity-contract.md) owns the inherited signature. Capture
texture, light, color, background energy, framing rhythm and stylized devices as observed. Do not
remove broad facial effects or comic timing in the name of realism; visual realism and expressive
exaggeration are separate choices. A changed look requires a scoped target decision.

## Persistent and scene-specific choices

Before finished drama asset and shot-image work, resolve the global visual manual in VISUAL and link it from Treatment.
Exploratory sketches or look candidates may help settle it; they are not accepted production assets.
Use the following fields when they affect this production, without creating a second style ledger:

| Decision | Global choice | Allowed scene variation | Reference or observable failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medium and performance | Photographed/stylized world; inherited acting and effect register | Motivated emotional changes | Source passages; no loss of broad reactions for realism |
| Art and materials | Location/costume/prop construction and surface response | Story-owned look/state changes | Accepted assets; no incoherent wardrobe or generic shiny surfaces |
| Photography | Light/exposure, perspective and attention/focus relationships | Location, time and camera-dependent projection | Comparable scene images; no conflicting light or unreadable evidence |
| Camera and editing | How framing, movement and cuts serve action/reaction | Scene-specific pace and coverage | Timed passages; no omitted reaction or arbitrary motion |
| Sound | Voice character, acoustic world, music/effect motifs and quiet | Distance, location and dramatic changes | Source listening or proposed cue plan; no masked lines or acoustic resets |
| Finishing | Known source/output color interpretation and intended texture | Necessary shot balancing | Processed test; no clipped detail, brittle edges or damaged skin |

Label observed evidence, inferred technique and chosen direction separately. A proposed direction
can proceed to look exploration; it cannot be called source-verified. In experience-led recreation,
Brief/Treatment can redesign daylight/night, camera or other forms through a tested replacement;
explicitly retained forms still bind. Genre/model fit informs feasibility and never silently
changes the agreed attraction or neutralizes required expression. Avoid treating any genre, light style or effort percentage as universally best.

Give the accepted direction a version and actual representative media. Record static and motion
status separately using [cinematic look](cinematic-look.md). Each scene states its local variation;
each shot inherits the applicable version. A change to the base look reopens affected assets, requests
and acceptance checks; a local change affects its dependents. Carry prior settled choices forward.

The base layer names the intended medium, texture, contrast, material treatment and color behavior.
Scene layers name motivated variations: time of day, location, emotional tone and physical lighting.
Project light direction into each camera as described in
[shot blocks](../production/shot-blocks.md#preserve-motivated-light). Keep a few accepted hero frames
as visual comparison targets. Record any intentional look change and its scope.

Do not impose 35mm grain, weathered surfaces, desaturation or anti-CGI text on every production.
Clean interiors, bright comedy, digital realism and stylized animation can all be intentional.
Choose exclusions only for an observed failure or actual Brief constraint, not a universal AI look.

## Motivated evolution across the work

Use the source [audiovisual grammar analysis](source-deconstruction.md#恢复随剧情发展的声画语法)
as evidence, then choose the target's rule in VISUAL. Record baseline → meaningful change →
scene/EX trigger → affected space/camera/light/color/sound choices → observable condition.
Motifs can recur with changed meaning; deliberate stability can express a relationship too.
Global consistency includes motivated evolution, not identical treatment at every beat.
Link the actual applicable palette/reference version and scope from shot rows. Do not create
new cards for every emotion or choose variations from a generic emotion-to-color table.

## Palette and reference allocation

Create and accept an actual global color-card image before new video, plus a local card for any
special scene whose colors deliberately differ. Every scene selects the current global card or
a named local variation with parent, scope and dramatic reason. No fixed swatch quota applies.
VISUAL records each color name, HEX, dominant/supporting/accent duty, concrete object/material
targets, approximate visual weight, lighting behavior and any semantic exclusions. Preserve
meaningful identity/prop colors; do not spread an accent from a costume onto every skin surface.

The deliverable contains a readable card image (editable SVG or raster) and a matching color
table. Inspect both, then verify representative scene images and H3 motion against them.
A clean authored swatch card needs no generative model; if rasterized for an actual endpoint,
retain the master and inspect the raster copy. HEX records intent, not guaranteed rendered
pixels under light, compression or displays. Card swatches/labels own palette only, not final
flat styling or composition. Scene/hero references carry the integrated color/material/light.
Revised global cards invalidate affected local cards/assets/requests and viewing comparisons;
a local revision reopens its consumers. [Preproduction snapshots](../production/short-drama-production.md)
freeze current card files, semantic colors, document versions and scene assignment before submission.

In a RunningHub H3 app with limited reference slots, prioritize identity, composition and relevant
state. Do not displace a necessary identity or layout input merely to meet a color-card convention.
Use a coherent composite or a concise palette description when it better serves the supported route.

## Expand rules in each independent request

Names such as identity rule or no-score rule help project organization. They do not convey their
contents to a model that cannot read the project. Resolve the current version and expand each
applicable rule in the actual H3 text, without duplicating contradictory instructions. Bind the
associated media through the real request. Do not insert private audit metadata into the H3 format.

For example, a rule named keep-class-present must become observable direction: students remain
seated in the established rows while the teacher and lead speak, with the same occupied background
zones in the reverse angle. Merely naming the rule does not transfer that fact.

## Keep the route and acceptance explicit

Record model/app configuration and seeds where exposed, without treating a seed as identity memory.
This installation generates video only through [RunningHub H3](../production/runninghub-h3.md).
Style assets remain reusable, but do not require a second video supplier or switch models for a shot.
Judge the actual shot and scene against accepted hero frames and Treatment; repair mismatches at
asset, generation or finishing level according to the observed cause.
