# Sound and mix

Read this when deciding what the audience should hear across a passage or the complete Film, and
how the work's existing sound relates to added music or effects. [Voice and performance](voice-and-performance.md)
owns who performs the words and where that voice comes from.

Before mix decisions, read [sound direction](../../creation/sound-direction.md). The cue hierarchy
comes from scene intention, not a fixed dialogue/music/effect rank. Keep those decisions in
the current STORYBOARD; actual adopted anchors and final listening close their realization.

Original source listening can be explicitly [deferred](../../production/source-audio-policy.md)
in a subtitle/visual-first commission. That does not defer inspecting the actual H3/edit sound.
Use the native performance as the base, retain useful sync effects, then add only selected score/
cues and mix repair. The [sound-direction disposition and post pass](../../creation/sound-direction.md#粗剪后的导演声音定案与执行)
owns retain/augment/replace decisions. A user's real basic listening can supply observations;
measurements and scripts do not impersonate hearing.

## Start with the sound the material already carries

In speaking-video A-roll, the person's picture, words and voice belong to the same performed Take.
Its sound continues at the current Timeline position while B-roll, graphics or a different view
changes the picture. Audio-only A-roll likewise supplies performed speech, with its picture authored
separately. A wordless performance may carry its own sound or be silent. Present the selected Take's
audio through [Sound](../../production/sound.md) according to what this passage should actually play.

B-roll can be a still, a silent video, or video with useful source sound. Its visual role alone does
not decide whether the audience hears that sound. An action or reaction shot might contribute a particular
door, footstep or burst of laughter; a picture covering a character's explanation may contribute no
audio at all. [Media](../../production/media-presentation.md#include-sound-deliberately) makes source
audio an explicit choice, leaving the selected performance voice available beneath a silent picture.

Listen to what the chosen material already contributes before adding sound. Music can carry pace,
mood or a change of thought; a short effect can give contact, reveal or transition an audible edge.
A sustained environmental sound can locate a place or connect cuts when the material itself does not.
These describe jobs in the work, not separate layers to fill. Environment may already be audible in
the performance; its duration alone does not make it a different kind of asset from an effect.
Designed quiet can be just as expressive as an added sound.

Hypit provides ways to prepare, place and mix audio, not a maintained stock collection of BGM,
short effects or long environmental sounds. Choose or make the particular material the work calls
for from supplied, recorded, generated or otherwise available sources and project Outputs. Treatment
captures why it belongs; Source and the chosen components express its use.

## Plan drama sound before generation

During source analysis and scene planning, record sound alongside its visual/semantic cause in the
existing detail, shot or edit rows. This is a cue plan, not a requirement to fill separate tracks:

| Cue/source detail | Dramatic job | Acoustic/delivery direction | Carrier and timing | Actual result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source interval or authored event | Voice, breath, contact, off-screen information, atmosphere, music or quiet | Speaker/register, distance, space, intensity and intended foreground/background | H3 performance/input where verified, retained sound or post asset; trigger/hold/end | Adopted version/interval, heard/missing/unverified and repair owner |

Carry vocal texture, accent, pauses, breaths and interruptions that contribute to the source;
do not manufacture a gasp or swallowing sound in every line. Plan the location's acoustic perspective
and meaningful ambience changes. Choose Foley for physical contacts and dramatic information that
need it; leave room for quiet and listening. A music motif needs a reason to enter, change, yield or
stop. No fixed number of layers or permanently full sound bed is required.

For RunningHub H3, [shot blocks](../../production/shot-blocks.md#choose-the-sound-and-performance-path)
owns the supported speech route. Send synchronous actions/delivery in the mode's timed description,
continuing ambience in `overall_soundscape` and audience-only score in `non_diegetic_music`.
Record which cues the actual H3 request is expected to produce and which the edit must supply.
An unbound audio label or a no-score instruction does not prove the resulting audio is controllable.

After candidate adoption, locate cues against actual audible/visible events rather than planned
block seconds. Inspect returned sound before adding or replacing layers. If speech, score and
effects arrive mixed together, do not claim isolated stems; verify any extraction for voice damage
and residual sound. Keep useful native sound, avoid doubled contacts/ambience/music, and repair at
the appropriate owner. Ordinary sound editing does not authorize external generative lip-sync.

Listen to the assembled passage for articulation, word emphasis, breath and reaction timing,
contact sync, distance/room continuity, music competition and designed quiet. Recheck after picture
trims or retiming. Transcription and loudness checks support this review but cannot award it;
unheard media remains unverified. [Post-finishing](../../production/post-finishing.md#sound-finishing)
owns final mix/export checks. A cue recorded in a plan is not evidence it survived in the film.

## Shape what leads the ear

When speech carries the scene, let consequential words be understood. A purposeful masked word
or interruption instead needs an explicit story/reveal condition. Do not automatically force all
other sound under every line; decide what the audience should attend to in that beat.
Music and effects can yield around a name, number, claim, punchline or quiet reaction, then take
more space in a pause or a passage led by action or music. Set that relationship through level,
placement, fades, arrangement and the selected source, rather than assuming every moment needs the
same balance. Normalization prepares a source; the audible balance is decided in the composition.

Give each added sound an expressive reason. A comparison can share a motif; a reveal may earn one
precise accent. A tactile process may be better carried by its small contact sounds than by a large
impact. Repeating an effect because another cut occurred can exhaust its meaning; variation is useful
when the event's dramatic role changes. Let the piece's character guide the palette.

## Attach sound to the event it answers

A sound responding to spoken meaning can follow a Script Selection or Moment, so it moves with the
accepted performance. A sound belonging to a component's own entrance, state change or handoff can
follow that event within the component. Music or another independently directed event can occupy
an authored span of the Film. Choose the time relationship from what makes the sound happen; the
same visual and audible event can share a cause without being forced into separate timing schemes.

The owner follows that relationship. [Sound](../../production/sound.md) presents audio from placed
Takes; [Audio Track](../../production/audio-presentation.md) places independent audio; Media or a
project component can publish sound coupled to its own picture and events. Film includes the wanted
audio contributions explicitly. These are composition choices, while the installed packages own
their exact playback, trigger and gain syntax.

## Preserve continuity across picture changes

A picture cut need not cut the acoustic world. Room tone can continue across views of one place,
music can bind a montage, and a speaking Take can remain audible as another picture takes the frame.
A change of place, speaker perspective or narrative state may instead call for an audible handoff
even when the picture changes gently. Follow the listener's sense of the encounter.

Let speech, source action and effect tails meet in the rhythm the scene needs. Use silence, fades or
overlaps where they make that handoff audible. Review the actual Film where these sounds coexist:
the chosen voice remains clear when it carries meaning, an effect belongs to its event, and the
soundtrack retains the work's intended character across the passage.

## Music, quiet and native sound are choices

Brief a cue by its dramatic function, entry/turn/exit and intended residue before genre, BPM or
instrument names. A repeated motif may evolve or disappear as meaning changes; a generic comic
loop is not the default bed for fear, intimacy and reversal. The source/Brief can also require
no score, source music, deliberate native H3 music or a music-led scene.

Keep useful H3 dialogue, breath, physical sound and atmosphere. If they are mixed together,
do not claim clean stems or mute the clip to remove unwanted score while destroying performance.
Use extraction only when verified undamaged, or repair the relevant H3 passage/coverage within
scope. Choose one owner for each cue to prevent doubled ambience, footfalls or music.

Define near-quiet versus full silence by what remains audible and its purpose. Keep the pause
where the audience needs expectation, embarrassment, shock or aftermath; no fixed silence
count/duration or permanent room-tone requirement applies. Level automation, ducking, reverb,
filtering and limiting follow that purpose and actual playback, not template constants.
